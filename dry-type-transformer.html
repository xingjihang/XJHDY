Why dry type

When oil is not an option

Inside the building

Oil-filled units need a fire-rated compartment, a bund wall sized for 110% of the oil volume, and often a deluge system. A dry-type unit sits in the electrical room next to the LV boards and passes fire inspection without any of that.

Basements and tunnels

No oil means no sump, no drainage, no oil–water separator. For metro stations, basement plant rooms and underground car parks this is usually the deciding factor.

Low maintenance

No oil sampling, no dissolved gas analysis, no tap-changer oil. Maintenance is a vacuum clean of the coil surfaces and a torque check on the connections once a year.

Where oil still wins: above roughly 2500 kVA, or where the ambient is over 40 °C with poor ventilation and the budget is tight. Oil-immersed S11/S13 units cost 30–45% less per kVA at that size. We supply those too — tell us the application and we will tell you honestly which one you should be buying.

Transformer questions

SCB10 or SCB13 — is the price difference worth it?

SCB13 uses a lower-loss core. It costs roughly 10–15% more and cuts no-load loss by about 25–30%. If the transformer is energised 24/7 — which most are — the saving on the electricity bill usually repays the difference in three to four years. If the unit only runs one shift, SCB10 is the better buy.

Can it be installed outdoors?

Yes, with the IP54 enclosure and a sun shield. But most buyers put it in a small prefabricated kiosk instead, because IP54 forces you into a sealed box with forced ventilation, and the fan is the first thing to fail. Tell us the site conditions and we will recommend which.

What ambient temperature is it rated for?

Standard rating is 40 °C maximum ambient. For Gulf and West African sites we normally derate by 5–10% or specify Class H insulation, because a 45 °C plant room with a 100 K temperature rise will otherwise age the resin faster than the 30-year design life suggests.

Do you supply the LV switchgear to match?

Yes. The transformer and the LV main board are usually quoted together, because the busbar rating, the incomer frame size and the cable box dimensions all have to agree. Quoting them separately is where interface problems come from.

What is the lead time and how is it packed?

20–30 days for standard ratings up to 1600 kVA, 30–40 days above that. Units ship in a plywood crate on a steel skid, with the coil protected by a moisture barrier and desiccant. Sea freight to Jebel Ali is about 20–25 days, to Lagos 28–35 days.
