Choose the frame type

Fixed or withdrawable

GGD2 · fixed type

Breakers bolted to the busbar, door-mounted. Cheapest per bay, fastest to build, and perfectly adequate for most distribution boards. You isolate a feeder by opening the breaker and racking out the fuse — you do not remove the breaker.

GCK / GCS · withdrawable

Each feeder sits in a drawer that racks out for maintenance without touching the busbar. Costs 20–40% more than GGD2, and is worth it where downtime per feeder is expensive — production lines, data halls, hospitals.

MNS · modular

ABB-compatible structure, 25 mm module. Preferred by petrochemical and power plant clients whose specification library is already written around it. Supplied on request.

Before you ask for a price

Seven things we need to quote a board

Give us these and the quote comes back the same day and is actually buildable. Missing any of them and someone will be guessing — usually the person who loses money when the board arrives and does not fit.

  1. Single-line diagram — a photo of a hand sketch is fine.
  2. Incomer rating and short-circuit current at the board terminals.
  3. Number of outgoing ways and the rating of each.
  4. Breaker brand and breaking capacity — or say "your recommendation".
  5. Busbar current and Icw — usually fixed by the upstream transformer.
  6. IP rating and installation — indoor, outdoor kiosk, dusty, humid.
  7. Cable entry — top or bottom, and the cable sizes coming in.
Buying the transformer and the board together? We quote them as one package, because the incomer frame size, the busbar rating and the cable box all depend on the transformer's secondary current. Two suppliers who never speak to each other is how you end up with a 2500 A board fed by a cable that will not fit the lugs.

We also supply the automatic power factor correction panel (GGJ) that usually sits at the end of the same board — 100 to 600 kvar, with detuned reactors if there are VFDs on site.

Switchgear questions

Can you build to my consultant's specification?

Yes — that is most of what we do. Send the specification section and the SLD, and we will either confirm compliance or list the deviations in writing before you place the order. Deviations stated up front are not a problem; deviations discovered at site are.

Do you provide a type test report?

For standard GGD2, GCK and GCS configurations we hold design verification to IEC 61439 and can supply the report. For a bespoke arrangement the assembly is verified by comparison and routine-tested, which is what the standard allows — but if your client insists on a full type test of the exact arrangement, that is a separate cost and lead time. Tell us early.

What is the difference between Form 2b and Form 4b?

Form describes how much is separated inside the enclosure. Form 2b separates the busbars from the functional units; Form 4b separates the busbars, the functional units and the terminals of each outgoing circuit from each other. Form 4b costs more and is what you want where a fault on one feeder must not take out its neighbours, or where live maintenance on one feeder is expected.

Can I buy the empty enclosure and fit my own breakers?

Yes, and it is a legitimate way to save money if you have a local panel builder. But the busbar, the short-circuit withstand and the internal separation then become your responsibility, and the CE declaration becomes yours too. We can supply the enclosure with busbar pre-fitted to make that easier.

How is it shipped?

Assembled bays are bolted to a timber pallet, wrapped and crated, and shipped as breakbulk or in a 40 ft HC container depending on bay count. A typical 8-bay GGD2 board fits in one 40 ft HC. Busbars are shipped installed; breakers above 1600 A are sometimes shipped loose to avoid transport damage and fitted on site.
