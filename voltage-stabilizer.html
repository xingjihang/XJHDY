The problem it solves

What a 15% voltage drop actually costs you

Motors draw more current to deliver the same torque at lower voltage. That current turns into heat in the windings. A 15% sustained undervoltage can raise motor winding temperature by 30–40 °C, and every 10 °C above rated class roughly halves insulation life.

The other direction is just as expensive: overvoltage destroys contactor coils, power supplies, VFD DC buses and LED drivers long before the motor notices anything.

A stabilizer sits in front of the load and corrects both directions continuously, so the equipment downstream sees a clean 380/400/415 V regardless of what the utility is doing.

Rule of thumb for sizing: add up the nameplate kVA of the loads that run simultaneously, then add 20–30% for motor starting inrush. If the load is mostly motors on a single feeder, size on the largest motor's starting current plus the running load — not on the sum of nameplates.

Send us the load list and we will size it for you before quoting. Oversizing a stabilizer is the most common way buyers waste 40% of the budget.

Stabilizer questions we get every week

Servo type or static type — which should I buy?

Servo is cheaper, more robust in dusty and hot environments, and handles overload better. Static (IGBT / thyristor) responds in under 40 ms, which matters for medical equipment, laboratory instruments and sensitive PLCs. If your load is motors and pumps, buy servo. If your load is electronics, buy static.

Can it run on a diesel generator?

Yes, but size it for the generator's kVA, not the load's, and tell us it is a generator supply so we can set the input range wider and add a frequency-tracking option. Gensets typically swing ±8% in voltage and ±2 Hz in frequency, both of which a standard unit will chase unnecessarily.

Does it work with unbalanced three-phase supply?

Frames from 50 kVA up regulate each phase independently, so an unbalanced utility supply is corrected per phase. Smaller three-phase units use a single three-phase regulating element and will pass imbalance through — tell us if your supply is badly unbalanced.

What is the efficiency loss?

About 2% at rated load for servo type, 3% for static. On a 100 kVA unit running at 60% load that is roughly 1.2 kW of heat, which is why the enclosure needs ventilation and why we do not recommend fitting a stabilizer inside a sealed room without an extract fan.

Can I get a weatherproof unit for outdoor installation?

Yes — IP54 with a sun shield and forced ventilation is a standard option and is what most African and Middle East site installations use. Add roughly 8–12% to the unit price.
